## 1. The choice

- **IR-20 · Three layers.** [ours]
  - IR-20.1 The setup rules (IR-1 to IR-19, IR-32 to IR-38 and IR-41) hold for the whole run, and prevent; the gates of IR-21 block inside the run; the post-hoc audit of IR-22 measures after export, and never blocks or feeds a run [ours].
  - IR-20.2 A failure of the setup halts the run with its class (IR-35): an integrity halt is terminal, and the run stays in every denominator; an infrastructure halt resumes (IR-34). A refused access by agent code is logged and handed to G2 [ours].
- **IR-21 · The in-run gates.** Each is a parameter value of the stage primitive (IR-40, in the index), with the class, hook and failure branch below [ours].

| Gate | Class | Hook, and why there | On failure |
|---|---|---|---|

| G4, references | setup (lookups), LLM filter (near misses), LLM fixer (the writer) | at the hooks U-INT-3 sets, and in the tail after the final fill, since the final paper is the one exported | unresolved entries block export, and the writer replaces them, as §4.2 has the Writer Agent "ground and correct the bibliography" [§4.2] [ours] |
| G5, method–code alignment | LLM filter, LLM fixer | at the hooks U-INT-3 sets, and in the tail after the final fill | the writer corrects the method text, never the code, as §4.2's Writer Agent uses the audit report "to rectify any discrepancies in the method section" [§4.2] [ours] |

  - IR-21.1 G2 discards by default. Its retry bound and its exhaustion value before the freeze are task 2's (U-INT-1); a G2 retry, if allowed, uses up a refinement (IR-40.1) [ours].
  - IR-21.2 G3's invariant: every number that a manuscript presents as a measurement, in its text, its tables or its figures, is bound to a verified-table cell and equals it. Its mechanism: engine code inserts the cell the writer names, and renders every plotted value from records; every other number is declared, and checked against the manifest or the row's configuration [ours].
  - IR-21.3 G4 resolves each bibliography entry through bibliographic APIs, as setup; an LLM judges only the near misses, at its IR-31 rates [ours].
  - IR-21.4 G5's checker reads the code, the task rules and the manuscript (IR-27.1); its fixer edits the method text only (IR-28) [ours].
  - IR-21.5 G1, G6 and G7 are hook names of IR-6.3, IR-14.1 and IR-17.1, not rules of their own [ours].
  - IR-21.6 In the tail, a gate still failing when its limit is spent ends the run without export, as a failure (IR-39.3, IR-40.2); before the freeze, exhaustion is task 2's [ours].
- **IR-22 · The post-hoc audit.** [ours]
  - IR-22.1 It runs once per audit identity, (bundle hash, configuration hash) (IR-36), after export: on the exported bundle, which it reads and cannot write; on the run's closed ledger; on the task-scope entries of the task; and on the run registry [ours].
  - IR-22.2 It applies the four checks as ScientistOne's §5 defines them, except I1's scope (IR-23), with our runner and the task's scoring items as I1's golden evaluator [Ref: meng2026scientistone §5] [ours].
  - IR-22.3 Its classes: I1's extraction of the reported numbers, I2, I4 and I3's near-miss judgment are post-hoc LLM audit; I1's re-fits and comparisons, I3's lookups and IR-24's checks are setup [ours].
  - IR-22.4 It writes only to the audit store and the audit ledger (IR-30.2); the bundle's hash is the same before and after it; and none of its outputs reaches a run (IR-29) [ours].
  - IR-22.5 Beside the four checks it reports a fifth, native number: the share of the exported paper's quantitative claims that map to released records through the provenance links (IR-24 (c)). Its precedent is ScientistOne's native Claim Provenance Rate, which its §5 defines beside the four checks and its §6.2 reports apart from them; so the four stay comparable with Table 7 [Ref: meng2026scientistone §5, §6.2] (ref:2605.26340v1:sections/05_coe_audit.tex:48) (ref:2605.26340v1:sections/06f_native_cpr.tex:3-8) [Tab. 7] [ours].
- **IR-23 · What I1 re-fits, and how it compares.** Its scope and its re-fit are ours, not the definition's; they settle A-ART-3 and U-ART-6 for U-NOTE-4 [ours].
  - IR-23.1 I1 takes the reported numbers from the exported paper itself, as the definition does, in which "The paper's reported score is extracted by LLMs" from its TeX and its PDF, and never from the engine's provenance links [Ref: meng2026scientistone §5] (ref:2605.26340v1:sections/05_coe_audit.tex:25) [ours].
  - IR-23.2 Its scope is every reported row at every report seed. Each recorded fit job in a frozen row's lineage is re-fitted once from its recorded inputs, at its code hash, job arguments and report seed, in a fresh environment of the manifest's machine class, and the re-fitted artifact is scored on report; a row is verified when every link of its lineage is [ours].
  - IR-23.3 I1 compares each value, and re-derives each reported paired gain from its re-fits, per report seed against E_base's re-fit at the same seed [ours].
  - IR-23.4 Its tolerance is calibrated per row. A row's σ_r is the standard deviation of the difference between two same-seed fits of that row: the audit fits the row a second time at c calibration seeds from the report list (c in the audit's configuration), on the manifest's machine class. σ_r is never set below the manifest's floor for the row's kind of fit, measured at packaging on a trained reference fit for trained rows, so that a frozen, deterministic E_base, whose σ_r may be 0, never sets a trained row's tolerance. A value passes within z·σ_r, where z makes an honest row's chance of any flag over its n comparisons (report seeds × settings) equal to 0.0027: z = 3 for n = 1, 3.46 for n = 5. A paired gain's report-minus-re-fit difference has variance σ_o² + σ_b² − 2·cov(o, b); the covariance is estimated from the calibration seeds at which both rows were re-fitted, as on a shared backbone, and where it cannot be, the tolerance uses σ_o + σ_b, which bounds every covariance. The tolerance of a mean over s seed pairs divides by √s only for pairs whose differences are independent. A gain that is not larger than its tolerance is reported as not verifiable [ours].
  - IR-23.5 Each row's audit budget is fixed before the audit, as a multiple of its recorded fit and scoring time that the audit's configuration sets. A row that cannot be re-fitted within its budget is reported as not verified, with the reason, never as passed [ours].
  - IR-23.6 A headline-only count, the definition's single score for *ours*, is reported beside the count over every row, for comparison with Table 7 [Tab. 7] [ours].
  - IR-23.7 A re-run of the scoring alone is not I1. Once the runner computes every number, what is left to prove is that each artifact comes from its code and that the paper's numbers are the records'; whether the code breaks the task's rules is I2's, since a hack can reproduce exactly [ours].
- **IR-24 · The audit reads the ledgers and the registry too.** Each check is deterministic, of class setup, and a failed one fails the task's integrity [ours]:
  - (a) no report job outside IR-14.6's four kinds, released or refused: a refused request is a violation that halted its run (IR-35.1, the index's section 7), and is counted here; released results are counted per identity, with the attempts beside (IR-33.5) [ours];
  - (b) exactly one freeze, and exactly one released result per test-event identity it lists [ours];
  - (c) every result number in the exported manuscript maps to a record of a frozen row through the provenance links, and agrees with I1's own extraction (IR-22.5) [ours];
  - (d) every reported row covers every manifest setting and every report seed [ours];
  - (e) each candidate's released search results stay within its counter (IR-13.3) [ours];
  - (f) every scored artifact has its recorded fit job, and its lineage resolves to the data its kind may read, pinned weights or recorded jobs (IR-3.2) [ours];
  - (g) the task-scope entries: E_base's admission records under the run's baseline key, and at most k sealed-check attempts (IR-15.5) [ours];
  - (h) the run registry: the run was registered in its campaign, under the campaign's configuration and guard configuration, before any test event of its task, or as a fresh start for a listed cause (IR-18.2, IR-38) [ours].
- **IR-25 · What a failed or unverified audit does to what we report.** [ours]
  - IR-25.1 It changes no artifact [ours].
  - IR-25.2 A task that fails I1, I2 or a ledger check is an integrity failure: a failure in every success count and in the failure-inclusive gain (U-EVAL-1), and in every denominator [ours].
  - IR-25.3 A task whose *ours* or E_base row is not verified, or whose gain is not verifiable, is not a success in the primary count, and has a column of its own [ours].
  - IR-25.4 I3 and I4 are reported per task. A person's review of a flag is recorded beside the automated verdict, never in its place: unlike ScientistOne, whose flagged I1 to I3 cases "were manually reviewed and corrected by human reviewers before reporting" [Ref: meng2026scientistone App. D] (ref:2605.26340v1:sections/012c_coe_audit_details.tex:54) [ours].

## 2. The attacks these rules stop, with the evidence

- **A reward hack that completes a task.** Without the specification filter the engine completed one more task, and "since the corresponding codebase contains reward hacking, it must be filtered" [fn. 2] (tex:sections/4_experiment.tex:43); an audit after the run would have counted it only after it had shaped the run. Stopped by G2 [ours].
- **A re-run that proves only determinism.** Table 7's first row scores 50/50 on score verification, with 1/50 specification violations [Tab. 7]. The one audit shown re-ran the trace's final script, which re-fits the method's statistics on ID-train and scores both methods, but reuses the fine-tuned backbone the two share and its cached features [p. 47] [p. 42] [p. 49] (image) (paraphrase). Stopped by IR-23's re-fit of every link of a lineage [ours].
- **Weights that a re-fit reproduces.** A fit that only loads weights from its own code tree reproduces them exactly, so a re-fit alone cannot tell where they came from. Stopped by IR-3.3 before any fit runs; IR-23 then re-fits what IR-3.2 admits [ours].
- **Paper numbers that are not the code's.** In ScientistOne's audit, mismatched and cherry-picked values make up 17 of the 22 confirmed score-verification errors, and its own system gates its draft on a claim check: "Only a draft with no remaining blocking violations is promoted to the final paper" [Ref: meng2026scientistone §4.3, App. E.1] (ref:2605.26340v1:sections/04.5_system_claim_writer.tex:20) (ref:2605.26340v1:sections/012c_coe_audit_details.tex:115-116). Stopped by G3 [ours].
- **An audit that sees only the final artifact.** ScientistOne's checks "operate on submitted artifacts alone and apply identically to every system" [Ref: meng2026scientistone §5] (ref:2605.26340v1:sections/05_coe_audit.tex:47). Its next sentence adds a native check for systems that record provenance as they write, the Claim Provenance Rate, "which measures the fraction of quantitative claims in the paper that trace to a matching entry in the experimental log" [Ref: meng2026scientistone §5] (ref:2605.26340v1:sections/05_coe_audit.tex:48); it covers the claims, not the steps that steered the search. The final X-Mahalanobis code shows neither the critic's test-set numbers, which we infer steered the redesign [p. 46] [inferred], nor the absent CIFAR-100-LT setting, whose place in the benchmark is an external fact (artifacts.md, A-ART-12) [p. 40] (image). Stopped by IR-24 (a), (b) and (d) to (h) [ours].
- **An auditor that writes into what it audits.** The reproducibility audit saved its verdict inside the task tree it audited, `./tasks/x_maha/logs/eval_result/repro_check.json` [p. 47]. Stopped by IR-22.4 and IR-30 [ours].
- **A gain inside the tolerance.** DMSQD's solution reports +0.61%±0.27 in mean QD over the reproduced baseline [Tab. 16]; a fixed value tolerance of max(1%, 3σ/|s̄|), ScientistOne's setting for its own runs, would pass a re-fit in which that gain vanished [Ref: meng2026scientistone §6] (ref:2605.26340v1:sections/06a_setup.tex:10). Stopped by IR-23.3 and IR-23.4 [ours].
- **An expensive fit that escapes I1.** ScientistOne counts a submitted artifact that "cannot be re-evaluated within the budget" as a confirmed I1 error [Ref: meng2026scientistone App. E.1] (ref:2605.26340v1:sections/012c_coe_audit_details.tex:118); the first version counted only failures. Stopped by IR-23.5 and IR-25.3 [ours].

## 4. Roads not taken

- ⛔ WHY NOT §4.2's arrangement for score verification, a prompt with no re-run inside the run: there the Coding Agent "is prompted during the experimentation phase to output self-contained, reproducible scripts and execution instructions", which a prompt cannot guarantee; §4.2's other in-run agents filter, look up and align, but nothing re-runs a result before the audit, by which time every decision has read whatever the run produced [§4.2] (tex:sections/4_experiment.tex:43) [ours].
- ⛔ WHY NOT rely on the audits alone for App. B's two failure modes, unmeasured trade-offs and optimizing the evaluator, which App. B says ScientistTwo "blocks both structurally, via a reproduction re-run (I1)" and two audits: an audit after the run measures, and cannot keep an evaluator edit from steering the search first; we block evaluator edits by the setup (IR-2.3, IR-6) and keep the audits as measurements [App. B] (tex:sections/appendix.tex:236-238) [ours].
- ⛔ WHY NOT run the CoE audit itself as a gate before export, as the register proposed: the engine would be optimised against the measuring instrument, and the gate's errors and the measurement's would be one and the same (the A-INT-3 file) [ours].
- ⛔ WHY NOT an I1 that re-runs only the scoring: once the runner computes every number, a scoring re-run is deterministic by construction and proves nothing [Tab. 7] [ours].
- ⛔ WHY NOT validate evidence tags that the writer types, as ScientistOne's Ground step does, which "validates each tag deterministically: the reported score must match the best-run score from discovery" [Ref: meng2026scientistone §4.3] (ref:2605.26340v1:sections/04.5_system_claim_writer.tex:7): a validator must extract the typed number and compare it within a tolerance; ScientistOne's own native check matched "within a 5% relative tolerance", and its 12 failures were "predominantly false positives of the extraction heuristic" [Ref: meng2026scientistone §6.2] (ref:2605.26340v1:sections/06f_native_cpr.tex:12) (ref:2605.26340v1:sections/06f_native_cpr.tex:16). Insertion by engine code needs neither a tolerance nor an extraction step (IR-21.2) [ours].
- ⛔ WHY NOT a value tolerance of max(1%, 3σ/|s̄|), ScientistOne's setting for its own runs: a gain below 1%, such as DMSQD's, could vanish in a re-fit and still pass [Ref: meng2026scientistone §6] [Tab. 16] [ours].
- ⛔ WHY NOT count a row that could not be re-fitted as passed, or leave it out: an expensive fit would then escape I1 and still count as a success [ours].

## 5. Relation to the paper and to the register

- **The paper: INCONSISTENT.** Three readings, with the reason the classification stands [ours]:
  - **Reading 1, §4.2.** The CoE audit "is a post-hoc evaluation framework" [§4.2] (tex:sections/4_experiment.tex:41). To guarantee its four properties, §4.2 says "To guarantee these properties, we introduce three dedicated refinement agents alongside careful agent prompt design": the filter discards rule-violating solutions "immediately after experimentation", and score verification rests on a prompt, "ensuring full reproducibility without requiring additional post-hoc refinement" [§4.2] (tex:sections/4_experiment.tex:43).
  - **Reading 2, Appendix B.** "Optimizing a single registered number exposes two classic failure modes", unmeasured trade-offs and optimizing the evaluator; "both" in the next claim refers to these two, which ScientistTwo "blocks both structurally, via a reproduction re-run (I1)", a protocol-immutability audit (I2) and a method–code alignment audit (I4), "rather than a prompt-level list of prohibitions" [App. B] (tex:sections/appendix.tex:229-239). Table 15 marks modifying the evaluation protocol as forbidden (audit) for ScientistTwo [Tab. 15] (tex:sections/appendix.tex:186), and Table 16 says "Our I1/I2/I4 audits make this class of edit inadmissible" [Tab. 16] (tex:sections/appendix.tex:313).
  - **Reading 3, reconciling [ours].** Blocks, forbidden (audit) and inadmissible can all describe the post-hoc audit's verdict, which rejects a result after the fact. Read so, the two places agree, and Appendix B overstates [App. B] [ours].
  - **Why INCONSISTENT stands [ours].** For I1 the two places name different mechanisms: §4.2 a prompt, with no post-hoc refinement, and Appendix B a reproduction re-run, set against a prompt-level list. Reading 3 reconciles the verdicts, not the mechanisms [§4.2] [App. B] [ours].
- **The delegated source.** ScientistOne's audit "is a post-hoc audit that checks whether claims in a completed paper are supported by the underlying artifacts", and its §5 says, in full: "While the CoE framework can support other evaluation forms—real-time verification during paper production or broader claim coverage—those are outside the scope of this work." [Ref: meng2026scientistone §5] (ref:2605.26340v1:sections/05_coe_audit.tex:16-18). The audit it defines is post hoc, and the framework leaves room for verification during production, which reading 2's in-run blocking would be [ours].
- **Our resolution [ours]: both, in separate layers with separate writers.** The structural guarantee that App. B claims comes from the setup (IR-1 to IR-19), not from an audit; §4.2's in-loop agents become the gates G2, G4 and G5; the CoE audit stays post hoc, as §4.2 and ScientistOne define it. Which side wrote pp. 47–50 (A-ART-2) stays open on the paper; in our engine the two kinds of verdict have different writers and different stores (IR-30) [pp. 47–50] [ours].
- **Where we depart.** One bullet per departure; what §4.2 specifies and we keep is cited to §4.2 in IR-21's table [ours]:
  - **G2's hook and branch.** §4.2's filter acts "immediately after experimentation" and is there to "detect and discard rule-violating solutions"; we keep both, run it after every result-producing unit of work, and, only if task 2 allows a retry, return the violated rule's ID to the author [§4.2] (tex:sections/4_experiment.tex:43) [ours].
  - **G4's detector.** Where §4.2 has "a search-augmented LLM identifies hallucinated citations", G4 resolves each entry through bibliographic APIs and asks an LLM only about near misses: ScientistOne's I3, used as an in-run gate [§4.2] [Ref: meng2026scientistone §5] [ours].
  - **G3 is new, and changes the writers.** The Paper Enhancer works by "revising narrative claims and updating empirical tables and figures" [§3.5] (tex:sections/3_new_method.tex:130); under G3 no writer types a measured number, in text or in a figure [ours].
  - **No counterpart in the paper.** The hooks G1, G6 and G7, IR-24's checks on the ledgers and the registry, and IR-25's way of counting integrity failures and unverified rows [ours].
  - **A person's review beside the verdict.** IR-25.4 keeps it beside the automated verdict, unlike ScientistOne's practice, in which reviewers corrected the verdicts before reporting [Ref: meng2026scientistone App. D] [ours].
  - **I1's scope and re-fit (IR-23).** §5 checks one score, re-running "the submitted solution on the golden evaluator"; we re-fit every reported row, so our score-verification counts are not comparable with Table 7's, and IR-23.6 reports a headline-only count beside them [Ref: meng2026scientistone §5] [Tab. 7] [ours].
  - **The fifth number (IR-22.5).** It follows a precedent in the delegated source, never a specification by reference: ScientistTwo's audit "comprises four integrity checks", and the Claim Provenance Rate is not among them [§4.2] (tex:sections/4_experiment.tex:41) [ours].
  - **§4.2's reproducibility prompt (P-INT-1)** is kept as guidance for the coding agents, and no rule rests on it: IR-3 makes every scored artifact re-fittable whatever scripts the agent writes [§4.2] [ours].
- **The register's proposal: half confirmed, half replaced.** Confirmed: the harness computes every result, since a re-run used as a gate proves only determinism. Replaced: the proposal ran the audits as gates before export and again at evaluation; here the gates before export are distinct checks by distinct sessions (G2 to G5, IR-26), and the CoE audit runs once per audit identity, after export, and only measures, so that it never becomes the target the engine is optimised against (IR-29). Superseded with it: note-check.md's account of the row, in which the harness re-executes each result before a critic reads it; the runner computes each result, and the re-fit is the audit's (IR-23) [ours].
